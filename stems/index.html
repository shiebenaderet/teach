The Five Moves

Five things a student can do to keep an academic conversation alive, each with the words to do it. Print it for the wall, or read it here before the lesson where you plan to teach it.

Use all five — then land it. That last move is synthesize.

Poster, 18×24 PDF · 7 pages Poster, 11×17 PDF · 7 pages

The stems travel; the examples don't. Every sentence stem below works in any subject — they're frames with blanks in them. The “sounds like this in class” lines are 8th grade US history, because that's the room they came from. If you teach something else, keep the stems and swap that last line for your own content.

01Conversation move

Paraphrase

Say their idea back in your own words.

Use it whenYou need to be sure you heard the idea right.

  • So you're saying .
  • In other words, . Did I get that right?
  • Your main point is .
  • So you think because .
  • What I hear you saying is .
Sounds like this in class

So you're saying the Constitution protected slavery without ever printing the word. Did I get that right?

02Conversation move

Clarify

Ask questions until the idea comes into focus.

Use it whenAn idea is fuzzy, too big, or only half-said.

  • Can you say more about ?
  • What do you mean by ?
  • Can you give an example of ?
  • How does connect to ?
  • What makes you say that?
Sounds like this in class

You called the Revolution radical. What do you mean by radical — and radical for whom?

03Conversation movePairs with Challenge · 05

Build

Add to a partner's idea so it grows.

Use it whenA partner's idea is going somewhere. Take it further.

  • I want to build on what said.
  • Adding to that, .
  • That connects to because .
  • If that's true, then .
  • Another example of that is .
Sounds like this in class

Building on that — if the government kept promising land it did not control, then the treaties were written to be broken.

04Conversation move

Evidence

Back the idea with something you can point to.

Use it whenYou have made a claim. Every claim needs a receipt.

  • The text shows this when it says .
  • One example from history is .
  • According to , .
  • That shows because .
  • This matters because .
Sounds like this in class

Document C says the mill hired girls as young as ten, which shows the opportunity Lowell advertised ran on the cheapest labor it could find.

05Conversation movePairs with Build · 03

Challenge

Push on the idea. Never on the person.

Use it whenYou see it differently, or the evidence is thin.

  • I see it differently because .
  • That's true, but what about ?
  • Have you considered ?
  • What evidence supports that?
  • Another way to look at it is .
Sounds like this in class

I see it differently. Jefferson wrote that all men are created equal and enslaved more than six hundred people. So what did he mean by men?

01–05How a conversation ends

Synthesize

Pull the whole conversation into one idea.

Use it at the endThe talk is winding down. Land it somewhere.

  • The big idea we landed on is .
  • We agreed that .
  • We disagreed about , but .
  • Our conversation showed .
  • So overall, .
Sounds like this in class

We disagreed about whether the Constitution was proslavery, but we both landed here: the men who wrote it chose not to settle the question.

Academic conversation skills, after Jeff Zwiers & Marie Crawford, Academic Conversations (Stenhouse, 2011). The five moves are Zwiers’ conversation skills; the routine for teaching them through guided oral practice comes from Project GLAD.
